Privacy Policy

Last updated 24 November 2026. Effective from that date.

Folio: Leather Day Planner is a planner app for iPhone. It is published by Faiz Rashid in Singapore, the maker of Kachingz. This policy says what happens to the things you put in Folio.

In short

We do not collect your data. Folio has no accounts, no sign-in, no advertising, no analytics, no crash reporting and no tracking, and it contains no other companies' code. We have no server that receives your planner. Your planner is kept on your iPhone. A few features send specific information to Apple, or to a website or service you choose, only to do what you asked; each one is listed below.

What stays on your iPhone

  • Your planner: tasks, notes, habits, scraps, events and time blocks you create, handwriting, focus sessions, your Private pocket and your settings are stored in a database on your iPhone. Folio does not sync it through iCloud or anywhere else.
  • Calendars and reminders: Folio shows the events in your iPhone's calendars and adds the events you create there. Those calendars sync the way you have set them up on your iPhone; Folio sends them nowhere. Reminders are read once, only when you choose Import from Reminders.
  • Health: when you link a habit to steps, workouts, mindful minutes or sleep, Folio reads that from Apple Health on your iPhone. It never writes to Health and sends Health data nowhere.
  • Your voice: when you hold to speak, speech is turned into text on your iPhone. The recording is kept only if you turn on Keep recordings of voice scraps. Voice messages and videos shared to Folio are written down on your iPhone too.
  • Photos, screenshots, documents and handwriting: the text in them is read on your iPhone. Photos are chosen with the system photo picker, so Folio sees only the ones you pick.
  • Search: Folio's search index is on your iPhone. Entries also appear in the iPhone's own search; a locked note appears there by its title only.
  • Widgets, Live Activities and reminders are made on your iPhone. Folio uses no push notifications.

What is sent to Apple, and when

  • Reading scraps and Ask Folio (Apple Intelligence). Folio reads your scraps (the things you capture or share into it) to suggest tasks, events and notes, and answers questions you ask from your own entries. This uses Apple's model on your iPhone. When a scrap or a question with the entries it needs is too long for it, Folio sends that text to Apple's Private Cloud Compute, which, as Apple describes it, uses your data only to answer the request, does not keep it, and does not make it available to Apple. We never see it. This happens on iPhones that support Apple Intelligence, without a separate switch in Folio; with Apple Intelligence turned off, Folio reads scraps with its own rules on your iPhone instead.
  • Weather (WeatherKit). Only after you tap Show the weather and allow location, Folio sends your location to Apple's weather service to get the forecast, and refreshes it about once an hour while it is shown. It sends the location you allowed: precise, or approximate if you turn off Precise Location for Folio in the iPhone's Settings.
  • Places and travel times (Apple Maps). When you look up a place for an event, the words you type are sent to Apple Maps to find it, with the area around your last known location if you have allowed location. When an event or suggestion has a place and you have allowed location, Folio asks Apple Maps how long the trip from where you are will take.
  • Apple Music. If you allow it, the desk page shows what is playing in the Music app; the album artwork is loaded from Apple.
  • Language and speech files. iOS may download Apple's language or speech recognition files that Folio needs. Nothing about you is sent with them.
  • Purchases. Folio Complete is bought through the App Store. Apple handles the payment; we never see your name, payment details or Apple Account. Folio asks Apple which purchases your Apple Account has, to unlock them.

Apple's handling of this information is described in Apple's Privacy Policy.

What is sent to websites

Link titles. When you save a link as a scrap, Folio visits that page to show its title and picture. The website sees an ordinary visit from your iPhone, including its internet address, as it would from a browser. This is on unless you turn off Fetch titles for links in Scraps settings; then Folio keeps the link without visiting it. Links you open from Folio open in your browser.

Your own AI service (optional, off unless you set it up)

In Scraps settings, Your Own Model lets you have scraps read by an AI service you choose, using your own account and key. It is off until you turn it on, and before anything is sent Folio shows which server will receive it and asks you to agree. When it is on, Folio sends that server the scrap's text, where it came from, and any link in it, for every scrap or only long ones, as you choose. Pictures are never sent. That service's own privacy policy then applies. Your key is kept in this iPhone's Keychain and is not synced. This setting is never used for Ask Folio or the Private pocket.

Locked notes and the Private pocket

Locked notes and the cards in your Private pocket are encrypted on your iPhone. The key that opens them is kept in this iPhone's Keychain and needs Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode. If you set up recovery words, they can open them too; Folio never stores the words. So that locked notes in a backup can be opened on your other iPhones, Folio keeps the backup key in your iCloud Keychain when iCloud Keychain is on (Apple encrypts it end to end), and otherwise only on this iPhone.

Backups and moving to a new iPhone

Folio keeps its last seven automatic backups on your iPhone. A backup you share or save goes only where you put it. In a backup file, locked notes and pocket cards stay encrypted, but the rest of your planner is readable by anyone who has the file, so keep backup files somewhere you trust.

Moving to a new iPhone sends your planner directly between your two iPhones nearby, over an encrypted connection, sealed with a key from the code you scan. It does not go through the internet or through us. The new iPhone sees the old one's device name while they connect.

Siri and Shortcuts

When you use Folio's actions in Siri or Shortcuts, such as reading your agenda or open tasks, Folio hands the answer to Siri or Shortcuts on your iPhone, which handle it as Apple describes.

Deleting your data

Delete any entry in Folio and it is gone from your planner. To delete everything, delete Folio from your iPhone: that removes the planner and its automatic backups. Backup files you saved elsewhere stay where you put them, and events you added to your calendars stay in your calendars; delete them there. After moving to a new iPhone, the old iPhone offers Erase from this iPhone. Because we hold none of your data, there is nothing of yours on any server of ours to delete.

Children

Folio is a general planner and is not directed at children under 13. It collects no personal information from anyone, including children.
